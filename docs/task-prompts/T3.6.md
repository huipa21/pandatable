## Scope

- Implement authorized Trash API and UI for soft-deleted workspaces, books, tables, fields, and views. Removed resources must disappear from normal navigation and resource APIs, while owners can find and restore them within the configured retention period.
- Restore the same metadata identities and physical data. In particular, restoring a table preserves its rows, fields, views, and attachments; restoring a field makes its values and field configuration available again.
- Add the daily purge job using `trash_retention_days`: purge expired objects, drop their physical schemas/tables/columns as applicable, remove associated attachment files, and delete their metadata.
- Pass AT-203, AT-325, and AT-610.

Out of scope: changing hard row deletion, undo/history behavior, retention-policy settings, and unrelated structure or attachment features.

## Contracts to respect

- Soft delete is reversible, not physical destruction. Keep physical names reserved until purge. Trash access, deletion, and restore must follow the existing scope-owner authorization rules; do not add an admin bypass.
- Follow D-129 for field deletion: retain the column/data and `default_json`; preserve the display-field reassign-or-fail behavior. Restore must not silently overwrite a live resource or bypass validation.
- Follow D-095 and `internal/ddl` conventions for physical DDL: caller-owned transaction, validated metadata identifiers, bounded lock timeout, and no `CASCADE` or `IF EXISTS`. Keep metadata and DDL consistent and surface failures.
- Purge only objects older than the configured retention cutoff (default 30 days). It must handle nested resources and attachments safely; a failed purge must be reported and retryable rather than appearing successful.
- Keep trashed resources out of normal nav/schema/query/ACL surfaces. Localize every new visible string in both `en` and `zh-CN`; keep Trash and restore controls keyboard/touch accessible.
